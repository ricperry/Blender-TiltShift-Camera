About these demonstrations

Blur comes from finite-aperture camera rays, without image-space blur, denoising, interpolation or motion blur. The videos add only a setting/value corner label. Focus, focal length, shift and aperture also exist in standard Cycles. The special lens movements rotate the sharp plane.

These ideal geometric optics examples do not simulate diffraction, shutter exposure or sensor noise. Video ranges are representative photographic choices, except for the full movement-axis cycle; they do not cover every valid optical configuration. Finite-distance focusing can change framing. Large movements can encounter singularities or invalid ray domains, guarded by the camera.

Photo pairs compare matched physical pupils where indicated. Standard focus/aperture fits approximate scalar blur rather than full image equivalence. Green overlays are optional, verified surface intersections of the exact sharp plane; they are not sharpness masks or depth-of-field boundaries.
